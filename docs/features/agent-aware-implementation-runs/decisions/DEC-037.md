## Context

DEC-036 lets the steps that write or deliver the change edit source and commit, and keeps the verification steps (tests, review, re-verification, CI checks, final verification) under the audit. The managed run adds "CI is green" as a verification step with no recovery, while the checklist's own instruction says "fix any failure". A failed check therefore paused the run at once: the step could only report, and nothing routed the repair to a step that may write (BUG-129). On 2026-11-25 Boris chose that both paths (the plan written directly in Cockpit and the managed run) are fixed in one release.

## Alternatives

1. Let the CI step write like a delivery step: the checker would repair what it checks and could report its own repair as a pass
2. Add a separate repair step after CI in the composed plan: a second step to approve and to skip when CI is green, with no bounded retry of the check
3. Leave the run to pause on the first failed check and ask the person

## Decision

The managed CI step stays a verification: it does not change files and reports each failing check as the blocker. MarkView gives it a bounded recovery (two attempts) that diagnoses the checks, repairs them on the feature branch, commits and pushes. A recovery attempt may change source and commit whatever Fix bugs says, because the verification then checks again at the new head. No other verification step gets a recovery. Fix bugs keeps its meaning (moving a bug into fixing) and stays off by default.

## Reason

Repairing a red check is a change to the branch, and a verifier that edits what it checks is no longer independent (DEC-036). Splitting the repair (recovery attempt, may write) from the check (verification, may not) keeps both, reuses the bounded recovery Cockpit already runs for a failed prerequisite, and confirms the result on the updated PR head. Alternative not taken: let the CI step itself write, which would break the independence DEC-036 protects.

## Consequences

The audit takes the attempt into account: a recovery attempt may change source and commit, its re-check may not. The step prompt of a repair attempt says so. A plan outside Build (written directly in Cockpit) is audited as before: its CI repair needs Fix bugs on, and the plan screen and the pause say so before and after a failure. Missing CI is handled by the recovery assignment (confirm it is absent; add a minimal workflow only when the goal covers CI), not by new evidence in the delivery probe.
