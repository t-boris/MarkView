## Context

Boris asked for the AI to review the experience of every step (how many clicks and scrolls a task takes, layout, fonts, an unnecessary stage) and to show the findings live, beneath the playing scenario, with a screenshot and advice. The earlier plan reviewed only key pages at the end of a run.

## Alternatives

1. Review only key pages after the run, as first planned.
2. Let the AI estimate the effort from the screenshots.

## Decision

The runner counts every interaction itself and records one screen state per step. Each new screen state, de-duplicated by a signature of its address and visible structure, is reviewed in the background: the masked screenshot, the counts, in-page measurements (text size, contrast, clipping, target size, labels) and the application's review requirements go to the AI, which returns findings with a proposed improvement. Findings are appended to a feed in the run view as they arrive. The review depth (every step, every new screen state, off) is the person's choice, with a cap on the number of reviews per run, and the cost is shown.

## Reason

The AI cannot count interactions it did not watch, and advice tied to one step is more useful than a verdict at the end. Running the review beside the scenario keeps the run as fast as it would be without it.

## Consequences

One more CLI call per reviewed screen state, so a long run costs more and takes longer to finish its review than to play; the depth setting and the cap bound that. The runner (S5) keeps the counts and screen states, the recorder (S6) keeps frames at step boundaries, and the review and the feed (S7) build on both.
